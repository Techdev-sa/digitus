Digitus 2D:4D
Measurement instrument · KSCDR

A finger is easy to see and hard to measure.

Digitus measures the ratio between the index and ring finger from a photograph of an open palm. The hard part is not finding the hand. It is that a photograph records projected length — tilt the hand a few degrees away from the lens and the fingers read short, and the error does not cancel between them. So the instrument measures the hand and the conditions together, and reports a ratio only with the spread it was measured to.

Method

Four landmarks, and everything around them

The measurement itself is two distances. Almost all of the engineering is in making those two distances mean the same thing twice.

  1. 01

    Photograph the open palm

    Twenty-four frames on the phone. Live guidance keeps the hand flat and filling the frame, and refuses to capture while it is not.

  2. 02

    Place four landmarks

    A model on the phone's Neural Engine finds the base and tip of the index and ring fingers. Bases follow the most-proximal crease, so two operators measure from the same place.

  3. 03

    Record the geometry

    The depth sensor records tilt, distance and how much of the frame the hand fills — the conditions that decide whether the projected length can be trusted.

  4. 04

    Re-measure on the server

    The same model runs again on the server against the exact pixels received. The phone proposes; it never decides the answer.

Refusal

What it declines to report

A plausible-looking number beside a spread of 0.45 is worse than no number: the reader has no way to tell it is meaningless. So the instrument withholds rather than guesses.

ConditionResponseWhy
SD > 0.08 no number Frames of one hand disagree far beyond the model's own spread — the hand was lost, not measured.
SD > 0.04 flagged Usable, but the reading varies more than it should. Shown with the caveat attached.
< 3 frames no number Too little evidence to call anything a measurement.
subgroup n < 5 withheld A median over two children invites a conclusion it cannot support.
Evidence

How closely it agrees with itself

Spread across the frames of a single hand, against the two benchmarks that matter. Shorter is steadier.

This is repeatability, not accuracy. It shows the instrument agrees with itself. Establishing that it agrees with a calliper needs the same hands measured both ways — which is the next piece of work, not a claim being made here.
